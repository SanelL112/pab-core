Introduction

Four step strategy to succeeding in this course:

Preview the content ahead of a class discussion on a lesson – I will provide a schedule for previewing OneNote lessons in the homework part of Canvas plans. Decide what resources help you learn the main ideas (videos, pictures, texts, etc) and take some initial notes in the provided templates. I like the
Cornell notetaking method
Links to an external site.
 as a way to write details and reflect on the larger ideas, but use whatever method you like.
 
Refine your notes in our class discussion of a lesson.  Maybe you had questions from your preview that you want me to answer. You can highlight concepts that I highlight or add to discussions that go beyond what you explored.
 
Finalize your understanding after our class discussion of a lesson. Now that we have fully discussed something, you can go back for a more in-depth reading of material. Hopefully you can answer the questions highlighted in that part of the unit study guide, or mark questions you cannot answer.
 
Review your notes and discuss with study partners. You know you’re ready for an assessment when you can talk about a topic without having to refer to your notes. This is crucial in this course because that indicates that you understand the concept rather than memorizing material (questions often ask about concepts in new contexts, so you have to understand the larger idea).


Profiles of student behavior

These behaviors do not necessarily guarantee the grade given below, but they may correlate with the listed semester score ("correlation does not imply causation").

Behavioral category

	"A" student behavior	"B" student behavior	"C" student behavior	"F" student behavior
Returning from absences*	Has already checked Canvas, completes makeup work independently, schedules tutoring if necessary	Checks Canvas about missed assignments, may be slightly delayed in catching up on work	Forgets to check Canvas for several days, falls behind on makeup work	Never checks Canvas, never thinks to makeup missed work or notes
 Note taking	Takes notes on videos prior to class discussions in own words, adds questions to be answered in class, adds to notes based on class discussion	Takes notes on videos (may sometimes be after class discussion on a topic), sometimes notes are just exact copies of my words (not showing much processing by student)	Some notes may be present from videos or class discussion, but note taking is inconsistent -- some material missing	No system for taking notes, may not even have materials in class (no paper, no pens)
Studying outside class	Able to answer prompts from study guide / vocabulary without referring to notes, able to have a conversation about a topic using their own words / understanding	Able to discuss some concepts without referring to notes, and recognizes other questions but may need to refer to guide to answer correctly	Gives robotic, memorized answers to questions -- if asked "what does that mean", cannot answer, other questions cannot be answered without referring to notes / guide	Unable to answer most questions, and unlikely to be able to find an answer in notes / guide
Studying in class	Works on assignments and notes without prompting to stay on task	Completes assignments, may need some prompting to stay on task, may copy other students" answers without thinking about material	Frequently off-task during assigned activities, may have "on" or "off" days, often copying study guide answers from others without thinking about concepts	Consistently off-task, present as a body in class but not present mentally

* This is especially important for AP students, especially those who miss classes to do college visits and / or participate in athletic / artistic events.  Please do these things, just keep in mind that making up work for AP biology may involve more time and effort than the average Lambert class.
