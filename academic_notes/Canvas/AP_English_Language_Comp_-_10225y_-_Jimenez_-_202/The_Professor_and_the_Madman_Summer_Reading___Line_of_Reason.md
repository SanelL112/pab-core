What is a Line of Reasoning?
A line of reasoning is the logical sequence of ideas that leads from a claim to a conclusion
It's the backbone of your argument in AP Language essays
Consists of five main components: claim, reasoning, evidence, explanation (commentary), and conclusion

Image from: https://www.teacherspayteachers.com/Product/Line-of-Reasoning-AP-Lit-Anchor-Charts-7199884


The Claim (Thesis): Your Argument's Foundation
 
A claim is a debatable statement or assertion that you aim to prove
It should be clear, specific, and arguable
Example: "Social media has a negative impact on teenagers' mental health because…"
Think-Pair-Share: Come up with a claim on a current issue and share with a partner.
.


The Claim (Thesis): Your Argument's Foundation
 
Non-defensible claim:
Social media has changed the way people communicate.

Defensible claim: Although social media allows people to communicate more frequently, it has weakened meaningful interpersonal relationships by encouraging superficial interactions and reducing face-to-face communication.
.

Reasoning: Connecting Claim and Evidence
Reasoning explains how your evidence supports your claim
It's the "because" that links your claim to your evidence
Strong reasoning demonstrates critical thinking and analysis
Example: "Social media negatively impacts teenagers' mental health because it creates unrealistic expectations and promotes constant comparison."

Types of Evidence
Facts: Information that can be proven through observation, measurement, or reliable sources
Statistics: Numerical data
Expert Testimony: Statements from credible professionals or authorities in a field
Examples: Specific instances that illustrate a broader idea
Anecdotes: Short personal stories or experiences that illustrate a point
Analogies: Comparisons that show similarities between two situations to make a point clearer
Historical examples: Evidence drawn from past events
Scientific Evidence: Data or findings from scientific experiments or peer-reviewed research
